Functionality & UI

Alcove's primary job is replacing and customising the new tab page,
which is what that category is for, and it's where new-tab
extensions are found. It also matches the single-purpose statement
you'll give on the Privacy tab - a reviewer reading "replaces the
new tab page" filed under "Workflow & Planning" may reasonably ask
why a planning tool needs a new tab override.

Language: English


================================================================
DESCRIPTION  (paste into Description*)
================================================================

Alcove replaces the new tab page with something calmer and actually yours: your own wallpaper, the links you use, and a set of small tools that open when you want them.

YOUR OWN WALLPAPERS

Upload your photos, or pick a built-in gradient. Blur and dim sliders keep text readable over a busy image, and shuffle rotates through your library on every new tab.

A CLOCK AND A SEARCH BAR THAT STAY OUT OF THE WAY

Search runs through your browser's own default engine, so results come back exactly as they would from the address bar.

SHORTCUT TILES

A row of the sites you keep going back to, under the search bar. One tile per site, so revisiting the same place ten times doesn't fill the row with it. Off by default, and it asks permission the first time you turn it on.

WIDGETS

Cards in the style of the ones on your phone, showing the weather, a clock in whatever time zone you need, or what's playing. Drag them to any of six anchor points, in square or wide. Drop one onto another and they share a slot, taking turns like a stack on your phone.

A DOCK OF SMALL TOOLS

The everyday sort: calculators and converters, clocks and timers, reminders and notes. There are a few odds and ends for developers too. Each opens in a side drawer instead of a cramped popup, and you can switch off whatever you don't use. The dock magnifies under your pointer and can sit along the bottom, the left or the right - or swap it for a grid that shows every tool by name behind one button.

NEWS ON THE TOPICS YOU PICK

Choose from the usual sections, or follow a subject by name, and the new tab shows headlines for those and nothing else. Each story lists its publisher and how long ago it ran. Read them in a panel, or switch on the ticker and let them scroll along the bottom of the page the way a news channel does. It stays off until you pick a topic, and the access it needs is asked for at that moment rather than at install.

EVERYTHING ON ONE SHORTCUT

Press Command K, or Control K, and start typing. Tools, saved links, widgets, the theme and every setting are one search away, so nothing is buried behind a menu you have to remember.

IMAGES THAT NEVER LEAVE YOUR MACHINE

Convert between JPEG, PNG and WebP, shrink something to fit a size limit, or open the editor to crop, straighten, grade the colour and add a label or an arrow. Paste a screenshot straight in. Every site that does this uploads your picture to a server first. This one does the work in your own browser, so the file stays where it is.

YOUR IP, WITHOUT THE DETOUR

Check the address the internet sees without opening a site that wraps it in adverts. It shows the network it belongs to and roughly where it lands, and it is the one tool here that has to ask someone else, so the panel names who.

REMINDERS THAT ACTUALLY REACH YOU

Type them the way you'd say them, like "call mom in 5m", and Alcove works out the time and shows you what it is about to schedule before you commit. Each one fires a system notification, and also appears on your new tab and as a count on the toolbar icon, so a Focus mode swallowing the notification doesn't mean you miss it.

MUSIC

Add a YouTube playlist and drive it from the new tab, with cover art and the full queue. No video, and it keeps playing when you close the panel.

LIGHT AND DARK

Follows your system by default, or pick one. Panels are translucent, and their tint follows the wallpaper rather than the theme, so they stay readable over a bright photo and over a dark one.

PRIVACY

Alcove has no server and no account. There is no analytics, no telemetry, and no backend belonging to the developer, so there is nowhere for your data to be sent and none of it is collected.

Wallpapers, notes and everything the image tools touch stay on your machine. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.

Alcove asks for as little as it can up front. Reading your browsing history for the shortcut tiles is optional and requested only when you turn that row on, and news access is asked for when you pick your first topic. A fresh install ships with five links already in the rail, so their icons are fetched the first time you open a tab; everything else waits until you ask for it.

Weather comes from Open-Meteo, which needs no account. Site icons are fetched once per site and then cached, and that can be turned off entirely.

Full privacy policy: https://player-html-ashy.vercel.app/privacy.html

The summary was rewritten as a sentence rather than a feature list
for the same reason the description was: the store indexes and
polices both, and the first version named five things in a row.

the toolbar tooltip and the extensions list - still shows the plain
brand rather than the longer store title.
